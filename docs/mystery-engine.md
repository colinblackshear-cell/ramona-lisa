# Ramona Lisa — The Mystery Engine (v2)
*Nov 15, 2026 · Colin · CANON. Replaces v1 (kept as mystery-engine-v1-superseded.md for reference only).*

## The promise
Every episode is a small history lesson wrapped in a comic mystery. Something important vanishes, Ramona and Kit bicker their way to the truth, and the object finds its way home.

Every episode delivers five things:
- **A real treasure with a real story.** The reader finishes knowing something true and surprising about it.
- **A vanishing.** Something strange happens, and the object is gone.
- **A Holmes-and-Watson double act.** Ramona and Kit bicker, banter and solve it together.
- **A hidden world.** Animal friends, informants, and an animal antagonist doing the dirty work for humans in the shadows.
- **A homecoming.** The object gets back where it belongs, sometimes so quietly that no human ever knows how.

Fill in the worksheet for each episode, run the checklist, and only then make the shot list.

## The ten rules
1. **One real treasure per episode.** A different object each time, with a story worth telling: a cameo, an amulet, a sword, a painting, a mechanism.
2. **The history is mostly true.** Use real objects and facts where possible. Where the story invents, such as a fictional cameo or an imagined owner, keep it true to the period and suggestive of the real thing. Note which parts are which.
3. **The history usually helps solve it.** Aim for at least one clue that depends on something learned about the treasure. That's the series' signature: the lesson pays off in the solution. When a story can't manage it, the history must still be part of the adventure, never a lecture.
4. **The strange event is real, and it misleads.** Something eerie is tied to how the treasure vanished, but it points the reader the wrong way. A second odd occurrence beside it is a red herring: a decoy or an innocent coincidence.
5. **Ramona is Holmes, Kit is Watson, with paws.** Ramona would rather deduce from her armchair. Kit does the legwork, keeps the casebook and spots what Ramona overlooks. They bicker constantly, and it's always comic, never mean.
6. **The bickering finds clues.** At least once per episode, an argument, a joke or Kit's distraction stumbles onto a clue neither of them was looking for.
7. **An animal henchman is in the way.** The antagonist is the menacing, sneering heavy who works for the humans: guarding their stash, running their errands, and blocking the pair at every turn. Funny and a little creepy. The humans behind them can be up to no good, but they stay in the background: a gloved hand, a phone call, a shadow at an auction.
8. **Research and friends bring the surprise clue.** Every case has a scene in an archive, a library or a back room, or a visit to a friend or outsider. That's where the unexpected piece usually turns up.
9. **Fair play.** Every clue in the final explanation has appeared before the reveal. The reader should be guessing, and able to win.
10. **A fun homecoming.** The treasure gets back where it belongs: a museum, a temple, a family. We don't always see how. What matters is a satisfying, funny ending: humans baffled, the henchman getting his comeuppance, the pair heading home bickering.

## Keeping readers guessing
- **Hide clues in comedy and action.** A clue dropped during a joke or a chase gets laughed past. On rereading, it was there all along. This is the most useful trick for a comic series.
- **Make one clue mean two things.** The walnut smell first seems to point at the kitchen staff, or at Kit's lunch. Only later does it point to ink.
- **Give every suspect a reason.** Each suspect needs a motive, an opportunity, and something to hide. Each secret explains some, but not all, of the strange events.
- **Let the strange event do misdirection.** The spooky spectacle pulls everyone's eyes, including the reader's, away from where the real theft happens.
- **Make the wrong answer convincing.** The false solution should satisfy for a moment. If the reader shrugs at it, the real answer won't surprise them.
- **Reveal by re-reading.** The best reveal makes the reader flip back: "That panel! I saw it!" Mark these panels in the shot list so the art can plant them carefully.
- **Leave a gap for the reader.** Just before the breakthrough, pause and let the reader guess. In the interactive version, that's the deduction question.

Length follows the story. About 40 panels is a starting point, not a limit.

## The rituals
| Ritual | Beat | The fixed shape | What changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| The vanishing | 1 | Something strange happens, then the treasure is gone. One hidden clue at the edge of a frame. | The event and the object |
| The red herring | 1–3 | A second eerie occurrence that seems to explain everything and doesn't. | Ghostly shapes, a curse, a stopped clock |
| The summons | 2 | Word of the missing treasure reaches the pair. | A pigeon's gossip, a coded note, a plea from the treasure's guardian, a newspaper notice |
| The pull | 2 | Kit finds Ramona comfortably settled and drags her out, bickering. Ramona gives in with "Get your vest." | Where Ramona is settled and what she's enjoying |
| The running bicker | 2–10 | A disagreement runs through the case. The solution settles it, and each of them was half right. | The topic |
| The look-in | 3 | At the scene. Kit: impressed. Ramona: "Is it?" | The scene |
| The henchman | 3–5 | First run-in with the animal heavy, who sneers, threatens and blocks the way. | Who he is and what he guards |
| The research | 4 | The pair dig into the treasure's past, or visit a friend or outsider. The history lesson lands, and a surprise clue turns up. | The helper and the place |
| The accidental clue | Any | Mid-argument, mid-joke or mid-snack, one of them stumbles onto a clue. | What they were bickering about |
| The human-sized chase | 5 | An ordinary object becomes a terrifying obstacle or an accidental ride. | Floor polisher, luggage carousel, rickshaw |
| The turn | 5 | An obstacle animal turns out to be scared, lonely or wronged, and joins them. | The guest ally |
| The wrong answer | 6 | Kit: "Case closed!" Ramona: "Not yet." | The red herring's secret |
| The trap that half fails | 8 | Ramona's plan goes wrong, and Kit's mistake or appetite saves it. | How it goes wrong |
| The comeuppance | 9 | The henchman gets a funny, fitting defeat. The humans behind him are glimpsed, foiled, or left for another day. | The defeat |
| The homecoming | 10 | The treasure is back where it belongs. We may not see how, but the humans' reaction is the payoff. | Where home is |
| The calling card | 10 | A small black-and-white card, or a single tuxedo hair, left at the scene. | What it says |
| The last joke | 10 | A rooftop exit, a final bicker, and food. | The food |

## The worksheet — Ep. 1, The Weeping Statue (worked example)
| Field | Ep. 1 — The Weeping Statue |
|---|---|
| Location | British Museum, London |
| The treasure | A sardonyx cameo of a Roman empress. |
| The history lesson | Cameos were carved from layered stone, so a white portrait stands out from a dark ground. Emperors spread their family's faces across the empire as propaganda. Every fashionable woman copied the empress's hairstyle, which is why historians date Roman portraits by their curls. |
| True vs invented | The cameo and this particular empress are invented. Cameo carving, imperial portraits and hairstyle dating are real. |
| Where it belongs | The museum's Roman gallery. |
| How it gets home | It is back in its case by morning. The curators are baffled; one black-and-white hair lies on the velvet. |
| The strange event | The empress's marble bust weeps every night at 12:17. |
| How it ties to the vanishing | The weeping draws every guard to the gallery, so the service corridor is empty. |
| The red herring | Pale shapes drift through the Egyptian gallery the same nights. They turn out to be museum mice dragging dust sheets to build a nest. The mice become informants. |
| The method | A walnut-ink reservoir and a clockwork valve hidden inside a hollow replica of the bust. |
| Animal antagonist | Lucien Vale, silver fox. |
| Human behind the scenes | A private collector, only ever glimpsed: a gloved hand, a phone call, a crate stencilled "T." |
| Suspicious but innocent | Barnaby, the badger guard, has been acting shifty. His secret: he hides in a broom cupboard every night at 12:17 because he's terrified of the "ghost." |
| The summons | Mortimer, a rooftop pigeon, brings Kit the gossip. |
| The pull | Ramona asleep in her attic armchair. |
| The running bicker | Whether Kit's stomach is a legitimate investigative tool. It is: the walnut smell cracks the case. |
| Research scene and helper | An elderly museum mouse who keeps a library behind the reading-room skirting boards. He explains cameos and imperial hairstyles. His offhand grumble about "those lazy modern copies" with their simplified curls is the surprise clue. |
| Kit's sense clue | The tear smells of pickled walnuts: it is ink. |
| Ramona's deduction | The bust rings hollow, and there is a seam behind the ear. |
| History clue | The bust's curls are simpler than the empress's hairstyle on the cameo's label photo. The weeping bust must be a modern copy. |
| Cold-open hidden clue | A silver tail slipping through the service door. |
| Guest ally (the turn) | Barnaby, once found in his cupboard. |
| Human-sized chase | A runaway floor polisher. |
| The trap and the half-fail | Ramona resets the timer to weep early, but Lucien bolts. Kit's flung packing strap, thrown while she's reaching for a pastry cart, brings him down. |
| The unmasking | Lucien is left trussed in the loading dock. The collector's crate goes out empty, with Ramona's card inside. |
| Calling card | "Returned to the empress. — R.L." |
| Last joke | Kit: "Never eating walnuts again." Ramona: "You said that about sardines." |

## Before any art: the checklist
- [ ] Will a reader finish knowing something true and surprising about the treasure?
- [ ] Are the invented parts marked, and do the real facts check out against a museum or academic source?
- [ ] Does the history help solve the case, or at least drive the adventure?
- [ ] Does the strange event mislead, with a red herring beside it?
- [ ] Could an attentive reader guess the solution from clues shown before the reveal, and are those clues hidden well enough that most won't?
- [ ] Is the false solution convincing for a moment?
- [ ] Does at least one clue come out of the bickering or a joke?
- [ ] Does a research scene or a friend deliver a surprise clue?
- [ ] Is the henchman funny, a little creepy, and properly in the way?
- [ ] Are the humans in the background?
- [ ] Is every ritual present?
- [ ] Is the ending fun: the treasure home, the henchman defeated, a last laugh?
- [ ] Is any culture depicted researched, and where needed reviewed by people from it?
